TEMPORAL RULE: I'm pretending this collection started 2025-01-01 and today is
2026-08-20. Each fact gets a "date learned" somewhere in that window. Most facts

a specific 2025/2026 news item or event, tell me the real event date so the
learned-date can be placed on or after it — never date something before it happened,

STYLE STEER (important — based on reviewing several hundred candidates so far): the
strongest signal for what I keep isn't topic, it's shape. I keep facts where a
specific agent — a person, company, or institution — provably does something
absurd, extreme, or numerically striking. I bin facts that are just passive
announcements ("a thing exists," "a thing was found," "a new species was
described") with no agent and no stakes, even when they're well-sourced and
genuinely interesting. Concretely, I keep things like: an institution doing
something absurd and provable (a company's weird legal clause, a government
loophole, a scheme that backfired); production-history war stories with a hard
number (a film shoot that went wrong, an album engineered to a strange spec);
engineering/web-dev absurdist humor; music-industry mechanics (not "why is it
called that" origin stories); counterintuitive science delivered as a vivid,
almost cartoonish image; vivid historical/literary scenes; accessibility features
that quietly work in practice. I'm sparing with plain "X was named after Y" origin
anecdotes — they usually get binned even in my favorite topics.

AVOID THIS SKEW: past batches have been extremely heavy on two things that almost
never survive my review, so please deliberately avoid them rather than defaulting
to them: (1) dinosaur/paleontology "new species discovered" stories sourced from
museum/journal press releases (NHM, Nature, Smithsonian) — I have seen dozens of
these and kept none; (2) generic passive space-object-discovery stories ("X was
found orbiting Y", "researchers found N new deep-sea species") with no human or
institutional angle. If you have a paleontology or space fact that's unusually
vivid or has a real human/institutional story attached, it's still welcome — just
don't default to the press-release "a new species was named" format.
